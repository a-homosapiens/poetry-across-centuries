Modern & Classic Poetry

A cross-century conversation

Ten modules trace the evolution of Western poetry from antiquity to the late twentieth century. Each pairs a foundational classic with the modern or contemporary poem that answers it — watching how structure, tone, and perspective transform as the world does.

The Ten Pairings

I I. Mythology Remixed

The Woman Who Waited

While Odysseus roams, Homer keeps his clever wife Penelope at her loom — described by others. Parker gives her the first-person last word.

The Vast “I” and the Hidden One

Two Americans, the same decade, opposite architectures of the self: Whitman sprawls outward to contain multitudes; Dickinson shrinks to a whispering “Nobody.”

 1855 ⟶ 1891 Whitman · Dickinson Enter module → III III. The Natural World

The Daffodils and the Mind of Winter

Wordsworth pours his joy into the flowers until they dance; Stevens insists you must empty yourself of all that feeling to see the snow as it really is.

 1807 ⟶ 1921 Wordsworth · Stevens Enter module → IV IV. Love and Anti-Romance

The Onion, Not the Rose

Shakespeare mocks the clichés of love poetry while keeping the sonnet; Duffy throws out the red rose and offers an onion instead.

 1609 ⟶ 1993 Shakespeare · Duffy Enter module → V V. The Cityscape & the New Rhythm

The Chartered Street and the Blues

Blake walks a rigid, rhyming London of “mind-forg’d manacles”; Hughes lets the syncopation of jazz and blues reshape the line itself.

 1794 ⟶ 1925 Blake · Hughes Enter module → VI VI. The Reality of Conflict

Glory, and the Lie of Glory

Tennyson makes a doomed cavalry charge sound heroic; sixty years later, Owen’s trenches expose the old ideal as a lie.

 1854 ⟶ 1920 Tennyson · Owen Enter module → VII VII. The Visionary Fragment & the Surreal

Fragments and Sleepwalkers

Coleridge’s Kubla Khan conjures a pleasure-dome from an opium dream, broken off mid-vision; Lorca’s ballad dissolves logic into the green dream-world of the surreal.

 1797 ⟶ 1928 Coleridge · Lorca Enter module → VIII VIII. Ruins, Time & Legacy

The Colossus and the Glove

Shelley warns the mighty with a shattered desert statue; Szymborska finds the same truth in a museum’s quiet display cases.

 1818 ⟶ 1962 Shelley · Szymborska Enter module → IX IX. Confronting Mortality

Defiance and the Dying Light

Donne stares down death with the tight logic of faith; three centuries later, Thomas answers with a roaring, grief-stricken villanelle.

 1633 ⟶ 1951 Donne · Thomas Enter module → X X. Mythology Remixed · the return

Giving Voice to the Monster

The course circles back to the Odyssey: Homer’s Sirens are a deadly lure heard from outside; Atwood hands the Siren the microphone to confess how the trick is done.

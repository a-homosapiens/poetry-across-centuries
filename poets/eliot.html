T. S. Eliot

1888–1965 · Modernist poet · Nobel Prize in Literature, 1948

“I have measured out my life with coffee spoons.”

Thomas Stearns Eliot was born in St. Louis, Missouri, moved to England as a young man, and eventually became a British citizen. More than almost any other writer, he defined the shape of modern poetry in English — fragmented, allusive, ironic, and haunted by a sense of spiritual emptiness in the modern world.

“The Love Song of J. Alfred Prufrock” (1915), written when he was still in his twenties, announced this new voice: the anxious, self-conscious interior monologue of a man who cannot act. Eliot went on to write the era’s most influential long poem, The Waste Land (1922), and later the meditative Four Quartets.

An enormously influential critic and editor as well as a poet, he received the Nobel Prize in Literature in 1948 and remains a towering figure of literary Modernism.

Federico García Lorca

1898–1936 · Spanish poet and playwright

“Verde que te quiero verde.”

Federico García Lorca was Spain’s most beloved modern poet and a giant of its theatre. Born near Granada in Andalusia, in southern Spain, he drew deeply on the region’s folk songs, Roma culture, landscape, and flamenco — fusing them with the daring imagery of European surrealism.

His 1928 collection Romancero gitano (Gypsy Ballads), which includes “Romance Sonámbulo,” made him famous across the Spanish-speaking world. His poems and plays — among them the tragedies Blood Wedding and The House of Bernarda Alba — are steeped in passion, moonlight, longing, and death.

At the outbreak of the Spanish Civil War in 1936, Lorca was arrested and killed by Nationalist forces; he was only 38. His murder made him an enduring symbol of art destroyed by political violence, and his work remains widely read and performed today.
